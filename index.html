Xiaoying Song

Ph.D. candidate & research assistant · University of North Texas

I’m Xiaoying, a Ph.D. candidate in Data Science at the University of North Texas, advised by Dr. Lingzi Hong. I received my M.S. in Information Science from Central China Normal University in 2023.

I build language models that collaborate with people, adapt to their needs, and respond reliably. Using multi-agent RAG, reinforcement learning, and efficient fine-tuning, I study when agents should ask questions, how to personalize their responses, and how to evaluate their interactions. My work connects model development with human evaluation in health misinformation, counterspeech, and crisis communication, with publications at NeurIPS, EMNLP, NAACL, COLM, and COLING.

Selected publications

All publications ↗
  1. NeurIPS
    Palette: A Modular, Controllable, and Efficient Framework for On-demand Authorized Safety Alignment Relaxation in LLMs
    Xiaoying Song*, Qitao Tan*, Arman Akbari, Arash Akbari, Yanzhi Wang, Xiaoming Zhai, Lingzi Hong, Zhen Xiang, Jin Lu, and Geng Yuan
    In Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems, 2026 Paper

    Problem: Blanket refusal behavior can block legitimate professional uses of LLMs.

    Contribution: Palette combines refusal-direction search, lightweight adaptation, and parameter merging for selective, authorized safety control.

    Key Insight: Domain-specific adaptations can be composed without retraining while preserving safety outside the authorized domains.

  2. EMNLP
    Ask or Answer: A Decision Framework for Multi-Turn Health Misinformation Intervention
    Xiaoying Song, Anirban Saha Anik, Jinyu Liu, Qitao Tan, Geng Yuan, and Lingzi Hong
    In Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing, 2026 Paper

    Problem: Asking more questions can improve a correction, but also adds interaction cost.

    Contribution: RO-PnR learns when to probe and when to deliver a final response in health misinformation dialogues.

    Key Insight: Weighing information gain against interaction cost improves utility with about 30% fewer turns than always-probe policies.

  3. EMNLP
    Speaking at the Right Level: Literacy-Controlled Counterspeech Generation with RAG-RL
    Xiaoying Song, Anirban Saha Anik, Dibakar Barua, Pengcheng Luo, Junhua Ding, and Lingzi Hong
    In Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing, 2025 Paper

    Problem: Health misinformation corrections may be too difficult for their intended audience to understand.

    Contribution: Controlled-Literacy combines retrieval and reinforcement learning to tailor counterspeech to different health literacy levels.

    Key Insight: Aligning language with audience literacy produces more accessible and preferred responses.

  4. EMNLP
    A Dynamic Fusion Model for Consistent Crisis Response
    Xiaoying Song, Anirban Saha Anik, Eduardo Blanco, Vanessa Frias-Martinez, and Lingzi Hong
    In Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing, 2025 Paper

    Problem: Automated crisis responses can vary in style even when individual answers are useful.

    Contribution: A style-consistency metric and two-stage fusion method that scores and integrates candidate responses.

    Key Insight: Selecting and combining responses at the instance level improves both quality and stylistic consistency.

  5. COLM
    Multi-Agent Retrieval-Augmented Framework for Evidence-Based Counterspeech Against Health Misinformation
    Xiaoying Song*, Anirban Saha Anik*, Elliott Wang, Bryan Wang, Bengisu Yarimbas, and Lingzi Hong
    In Conference on Language Modeling, 2025 Paper

    Problem: Health counterspeech needs relevant, accurate evidence alongside constructive language.

    Contribution: A multi-agent RAG framework combining static and dynamic evidence for health misinformation intervention.

    Key Insight: Coordinating retrieval and response refinement improves factual accuracy, informativeness, relevance, and politeness.

  6. NAACL
    Echoes of Discord: Forecasting Hater Reactions to Counterspeech
    Xiaoying Song, Sharon Lisseth Perez, Xinchen Yu, Eduardo Blanco, and Lingzi Hong
    In Nations of the Americas Chapter of the Association for Computational Linguistics, 2025 Paper

    Problem: A counterspeech response alone does not reveal whether a hater will return or remain hateful.

    Contribution: ReEco, a dataset of three-turn Reddit conversations, supports forecasting hater reentry and subsequent hatefulness.

    Key Insight: Counterspeech language is linked to later reactions, making conversational outcomes a useful evaluation target.
