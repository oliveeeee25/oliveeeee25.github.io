Publications

2026

  1. EMNLP
    Ask or Answer: A Decision Framework for Multi-Turn Health Misinformation Intervention
    Xiaoying Song, Anirban Saha Anik, Jinyu Liu, Qitao Tan, Geng Yuan, and Lingzi Hong
    In Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing, 2026
    Proposes RO-PnR (Reward-Optimized Probe-and-Respond), which decides at each turn whether to probe for more information or commit to a final correction, guided by a turn-level reward weighing a probe’s expected information gain against its interaction cost. Achieves the highest cost-adjusted utility across three health-misinformation datasets and three base models while using  30% fewer turns than always-probe baselines.
    human-agent collaboration multi-turn dialogue clarifying questions reward optimization
  2. Preprint
    Towards Human-Centered Natural Language Processing: A Survey of Cognitive Signal Integration
    Xiaoying Song, Anirban Saha Anik, Jinyu Liu, Qitao Tan, Geng Yuan, Song Fu, Eduardo Blanco, and Lingzi Hong
    arXiv preprint, 2026
    Surveys cognition-aware NLP: the datasets of cognitive signals such as eye-tracking traces and EEG, the preprocessing pipelines, the tasks they are applied to, and how they are integrated with language models. Summarizes open challenges and future opportunities for more interpretable, user-centered human-AI interaction.
    cognitive signals eye tracking EEG survey human-centered NLP
  3. Preprint
    ST-Crisis: A Diagnostic Benchmark for Spatiotemporal Grounding in Crisis Communication
    Xiaoying Song, Thi Bich Ngan Do, Nikhitha Rayabarapu, Anirban Saha Anik, Jinyu Liu, Qitao Tan, Vanessa Frias-Martinez, and Lingzi Hong
    arXiv preprint, 2026
    Introduces ST-Crisis, a benchmark pairing situational awareness posts with crisis need queries to evaluate spatiotemporal grounding and reasoning. A diagnostic evaluation of vanilla LLMs and spatiotemporally grounded RAG models exposes where current models fail to ground evidence in location and time during crisis response.
    spatiotemporal reasoning benchmark RAG crisis communication
  4. Preprint
    Palette: A Modular, Controllable, and Efficient Framework for On-demand Authorized Safety Alignment Relaxation in LLMs
    Qitao Tan, Xiaoying Song, Arman Akbari, Arash Akbari, Yanzhi Wang, Xiaoming Zhai, Lingzi Hong, Zhen Xiang, Jin Lu, and Geng Yuan
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2605.24154, 2026 PDF
    Proposes Palette, a framework that selectively relaxes LLM refusal behavior in authorized professional domains while preserving safety elsewhere, combining refusal-direction search, lightweight adaptation, and parameter merging for multi-domain composition without retraining.
    safety alignment refusal behavior model merging controllability
  5. EMNLP
    Q-realign: Piggybacking Realignment on Quantization for Safe and Efficient LLM Deployment
    Qitao Tan, Xiaoying Song, Ningxi Cheng, Ninghao Liu, Xiaoming Zhai, Lingzi Hong, Yanzhi Wang, Zhen Xiang, and Geng Yuan
    In Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing, 2026 PDF
    Introduces Q-realign, a post-training defense that restores safety alignment eroded by task-specific fine-tuning by piggybacking realignment on quantization. It reduces unsafe outputs while preserving task performance, realigning a 7B model on consumer hardware in about 40 minutes.
    safety realignment quantization fine-tuning risks efficient deployment
  6. EMNLP
    End-to-End On-Device Quantization-Aware Training for LLMs at Inference Cost
    Qitao Tan, Xiaoying Song, Jin Lu, Guoming Li, Jun Liu, Lingzi Hong, Caiwen Ding, Jundong Li, Xiaoming Zhai, Shaoyi Huang, Wei Niu, and Geng Yuan
    In Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing, 2026 PDF
    Proposes ZeroQAT, a quantization-aware training framework that uses zeroth-order, forward-only gradient estimation to avoid backpropagation memory costs, enabling 2-4 bit fine-tuning of a 13B model on an 8GB GPU and on-smartphone training.
    quantization zeroth-order optimization on-device training
  7. JIS
    Comparative analysis of large language models’ performance in book classification tasks using Library of Congress Classification system
    Xiaoying Song, Pengcheng Luo, Jason Thomale, Oksana Zavalina, and Lingzi Hong
    Journal of Information Science, 2026 Paper
    Compares LLMs on classifying books into the Library of Congress Classification system, varying input data, data size, model, and class granularity. Llama3 performs best; diverse inputs help, but models struggle with long text and fine-grained categories.
    LLM evaluation book classification library cataloging
