Personalized alignment & safety

Adapting model behavior to users’ literacy, conversational goals, and authorized needs.

  1. NeurIPS 2026 Paper ↗

    Palette: A Modular, Controllable, and Efficient Framework for On-demand Authorized Safety Alignment Relaxation in LLMs

    Xiaoying Song*, Qitao Tan*, Arman Akbari, Arash Akbari, Yanzhi Wang, Xiaoming Zhai, Lingzi Hong, Zhen Xiang, Jin Lu, and Geng Yuan
    Problem
    Blanket refusal behavior can block legitimate professional uses of LLMs.
    Contribution
    Palette combines refusal-direction search, lightweight adaptation, and parameter merging for selective, authorized safety control.
    Key Insight
    Domain-specific adaptations can be composed without retraining while preserving safety outside the authorized domains.
  2. EMNLP 2026 Paper ↗

    Q-realign: Piggybacking Realignment on Quantization for Safe and Efficient LLM Deployment

    Qitao Tan, Xiaoying Song, Ningxi Cheng, Ninghao Liu, Xiaoming Zhai, Lingzi Hong, Yanzhi Wang, Zhen Xiang, and Geng Yuan
    Problem
    Task-specific fine-tuning can erode safety alignment, making deployment riskier.
    Contribution
    Q-realign restores alignment during quantization, reducing unsafe outputs while preserving task performance.
    Key Insight
    Combining realignment with compression makes safety restoration practical on consumer hardware.
  3. EMNLP 2025 Paper ↗

    Speaking at the Right Level: Literacy-Controlled Counterspeech Generation with RAG-RL

    Xiaoying Song, Anirban Saha Anik, Dibakar Barua, Pengcheng Luo, Junhua Ding, and Lingzi Hong
    Problem
    Health misinformation corrections may be too difficult for their intended audience to understand.
    Contribution
    Controlled-Literacy combines retrieval and reinforcement learning to tailor counterspeech to different health literacy levels.
    Key Insight
    Aligning language with audience literacy produces more accessible and preferred responses.
  4. EMNLP 2024 Paper ↗

    Outcome-Constrained Large Language Models for Countering Hate Speech

    Lingzi Hong, Pengcheng Luo, Eduardo Blanco, and Xiaoying Song
    Problem
    Counterspeech generation should account for what happens next in a conversation.
    Contribution
    Prompting, fine-tuning, and reinforcement learning methods constrained by low incivility and non-hateful hater reentry.
    Key Insight
    Desired conversational outcomes can directly guide generation toward more constructive exchanges.

LLM agents & post-training

Learning dialogue decisions, coordinating evidence and responses, and making model adaptation more efficient.

  1. EMNLP 2026 Paper ↗

    Ask or Answer: A Decision Framework for Multi-Turn Health Misinformation Intervention

    Xiaoying Song, Anirban Saha Anik, Jinyu Liu, Qitao Tan, Geng Yuan, and Lingzi Hong
    Problem
    Asking more questions can improve a correction, but also adds interaction cost.
    Contribution
    RO-PnR learns when to probe and when to deliver a final response in health misinformation dialogues.
    Key Insight
    Weighing information gain against interaction cost improves utility with about 30% fewer turns than always-probe policies.
  2. EMNLP 2026 Paper ↗

    End-to-End On-Device Quantization-Aware Training for LLMs at Inference Cost

    Qitao Tan, Xiaoying Song, Jin Lu, Guoming Li, Jun Liu, Lingzi Hong, Caiwen Ding, Jundong Li, Xiaoming Zhai, Shaoyi Huang, Wei Niu, and Geng Yuan
    Problem
    Backpropagation makes quantization-aware LLM training too memory-intensive for many devices.
    Contribution
    ZeroQAT uses forward-only, zeroth-order optimization for low-bit training, including 13B models on an 8GB GPU.
    Key Insight
    Avoiding backpropagation brings training memory requirements close to inference costs, enabling on-device adaptation.
  3. EMNLP 2025 Paper ↗

    A Dynamic Fusion Model for Consistent Crisis Response

    Xiaoying Song, Anirban Saha Anik, Eduardo Blanco, Vanessa Frias-Martinez, and Lingzi Hong
    Problem
    Automated crisis responses can vary in style even when individual answers are useful.
    Contribution
    A style-consistency metric and two-stage fusion method that scores and integrates candidate responses.
    Key Insight
    Selecting and combining responses at the instance level improves both quality and stylistic consistency.
  4. COLM 2025 Paper ↗

    Multi-Agent Retrieval-Augmented Framework for Evidence-Based Counterspeech Against Health Misinformation

    Xiaoying Song*, Anirban Saha Anik*, Elliott Wang, Bryan Wang, Bengisu Yarimbas, and Lingzi Hong
    Problem
    Health counterspeech needs relevant, accurate evidence alongside constructive language.
    Contribution
    A multi-agent RAG framework combining static and dynamic evidence for health misinformation intervention.
    Key Insight
    Coordinating retrieval and response refinement improves factual accuracy, informativeness, relevance, and politeness.
  5. ISCRAM 2025 Paper ↗

    Dynamic Fusion of Large Language Models for Crisis Communication

    Xiaoying Song, Lingzi Hong, Anirban Saha Anik, and Vanessa Frias-Martinez
    Problem
    Crisis communication must address information needs with relevant, empathetic, and actionable responses.
    Contribution
    A comparison of prompting, RAG, and dynamic fusion of multiple LLMs for crisis-related social media queries.
    Key Insight
    Dynamic fusion improves professionalism, actionability, empathy, and relevance by combining model responses.

Human-centered evaluation

Understanding model outputs and user reactions through human judgments, cognitive signals, and diagnostic benchmarks.

  1. NAACL 2025 Paper ↗

    Echoes of Discord: Forecasting Hater Reactions to Counterspeech

    Xiaoying Song, Sharon Lisseth Perez, Xinchen Yu, Eduardo Blanco, and Lingzi Hong
    Problem
    A counterspeech response alone does not reveal whether a hater will return or remain hateful.
    Contribution
    ReEco, a dataset of three-turn Reddit conversations, supports forecasting hater reentry and subsequent hatefulness.
    Key Insight
    Counterspeech language is linked to later reactions, making conversational outcomes a useful evaluation target.
  2. COLING 2025 Paper ↗

    Assessing the Human Likeness of AI-Generated Counterspeech

    Xiaoying Song, Sujana Mamidisetty, Eduardo Blanco, and Lingzi Hong
    Problem
    It is unclear how closely AI-generated counterspeech resembles human responses.
    Contribution
    An evaluation of multiple generation strategies using both classifiers and human judges.
    Key Insight
    Differences in style, politeness, and specificity make AI counterspeech readily distinguishable from human writing.
  3. iConference 2025 Paper ↗

    Analyzing the language of rejection: a study of user flagging responses to hate speech on Reddit

    Sharon Lisseth Perez, Xiaoying Song, and Lingzi Hong
    Problem
    User responses that flag hate speech contain moderation signals that are difficult to identify automatically.
    Contribution
    A dataset of Reddit flagging responses paired with hateful comments, with transformer-based classification and linguistic analysis.
    Key Insight
    Distinctive patterns in user flagging language can inform hate speech moderation.
  4. Preprint 2026

    Towards Human-Centered Natural Language Processing: A Survey of Cognitive Signal Integration

    Xiaoying Song, Anirban Saha Anik, Jinyu Liu, Qitao Tan, Geng Yuan, Song Fu, Eduardo Blanco, and Lingzi Hong
    Problem
    Cognitive signals offer a window into language processing, but their use across NLP is fragmented.
    Contribution
    A survey connecting cognitive-signal datasets, preprocessing, tasks, and language-model integration methods.
    Key Insight
    Eye tracking and EEG provide complementary perspectives for studying more interpretable, human-centered NLP.
  5. Preprint 2026

    ST-Crisis: A Diagnostic Benchmark for Spatiotemporal Grounding in Crisis Communication

    Xiaoying Song, Thi Bich Ngan Do, Nikhitha Rayabarapu, Anirban Saha Anik, Jinyu Liu, Qitao Tan, Vanessa Frias-Martinez, and Lingzi Hong
    Problem
    Crisis responses must match evidence to the right location and time.
    Contribution
    ST-Crisis pairs situational awareness posts with crisis queries to diagnose spatiotemporal grounding and reasoning.
    Key Insight
    Both standard LLMs and grounded RAG systems still struggle to connect crisis evidence to where and when it applies.

Knowledge discovery & representation

Organizing information, learning representations, and uncovering patterns in scholarly data.

  1. JIS 2026 Paper ↗

    Comparative analysis of large language models’ performance in book classification tasks using Library of Congress Classification system

    Xiaoying Song, Pengcheng Luo, Jason Thomale, Oksana Zavalina, and Lingzi Hong
    Problem
    Book classification requires LLMs to navigate a detailed library taxonomy using varied source material.
    Contribution

  2. ASIST 2025 Paper ↗

    A Hybrid Framework for Subject Analysis: Integrating Embedding-Based Regression Models with Large Language Models

    Jinyu Liu, Xiaoying Song, Diana Zhang, Jason Thomale, Daqing He, and Lingzi Hong
    Problem
    LLM-generated subject headings can hallucinate labels or depart from a controlled vocabulary.
    Contribution
    A hybrid framework using embeddings to predict heading counts and guide post-editing of LLM outputs.
    Key Insight
    Combining prediction with vocabulary-aware editing yields more controlled subject assignments with fewer hallucinations.

  4. MLJ 2023 Paper ↗

    An effective negative sampling approach for contrastive learning of sentence embedding

    Qitao Tan, Xiaoying Song, Guanghui Ye, and Chuan Wu

    Contribution

    Key Insight
    More deliberate negative selection improves sentence representations across seven semantic similarity datasets.
  5. JOI 2023 Paper ↗
