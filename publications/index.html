Personalized alignment & safety

Adapting model behavior to users’ literacy, conversational goals, and authorized needs.

  1. NeurIPS
    Palette: A Modular, Controllable, and Efficient Framework for On-demand Authorized Safety Alignment Relaxation in LLMs
    Xiaoying Song*, Qitao Tan*, Arman Akbari, Arash Akbari, Yanzhi Wang, Xiaoming Zhai, Lingzi Hong, Zhen Xiang, Jin Lu, and Geng Yuan
    In Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems, 2026 Paper

    Problem: Blanket refusal behavior can block legitimate professional uses of LLMs.

    Contribution: Palette combines refusal-direction search, lightweight adaptation, and parameter merging for selective, authorized safety control.

    Key Insight: Domain-specific adaptations can be composed without retraining while preserving safety outside the authorized domains.

  2. EMNLP
    Q-realign: Piggybacking Realignment on Quantization for Safe and Efficient LLM Deployment
    Qitao Tan, Xiaoying Song, Ningxi Cheng, Ninghao Liu, Xiaoming Zhai, Lingzi Hong, Yanzhi Wang, Zhen Xiang, and Geng Yuan
    In Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing, 2026 Paper

    Problem: Task-specific fine-tuning can erode safety alignment, making deployment riskier.

    Contribution: Q-realign restores alignment during quantization, reducing unsafe outputs while preserving task performance.

    Key Insight: Combining realignment with compression makes safety restoration practical on consumer hardware.

  3. EMNLP
    Speaking at the Right Level: Literacy-Controlled Counterspeech Generation with RAG-RL
    Xiaoying Song, Anirban Saha Anik, Dibakar Barua, Pengcheng Luo, Junhua Ding, and Lingzi Hong
    In Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing, 2025 Paper

    Problem: Health misinformation corrections may be too difficult for their intended audience to understand.

    Contribution: Controlled-Literacy combines retrieval and reinforcement learning to tailor counterspeech to different health literacy levels.

    Key Insight: Aligning language with audience literacy produces more accessible and preferred responses.

  4. EMNLP
    Outcome-Constrained Large Language Models for Countering Hate Speech
    Lingzi Hong, Pengcheng Luo, Eduardo Blanco, and Xiaoying Song
    In Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing, 2024 Paper

    Problem: Counterspeech generation should account for what happens next in a conversation.

    Contribution: Prompting, fine-tuning, and reinforcement learning methods constrained by low incivility and non-hateful hater reentry.

    Key Insight: Desired conversational outcomes can directly guide generation toward more constructive exchanges.

LLM agents & post-training

Learning dialogue decisions, coordinating evidence and responses, and making model adaptation more efficient.

  1. EMNLP
    Ask or Answer: A Decision Framework for Multi-Turn Health Misinformation Intervention
    Xiaoying Song, Anirban Saha Anik, Jinyu Liu, Qitao Tan, Geng Yuan, and Lingzi Hong
    In Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing, 2026 Paper

    Problem: Asking more questions can improve a correction, but also adds interaction cost.

    Contribution: RO-PnR learns when to probe and when to deliver a final response in health misinformation dialogues.

    Key Insight: Weighing information gain against interaction cost improves utility with about 30% fewer turns than always-probe policies.

  2. EMNLP
    End-to-End On-Device Quantization-Aware Training for LLMs at Inference Cost
    Qitao Tan, Xiaoying Song, Jin Lu, Guoming Li, Jun Liu, Lingzi Hong, Caiwen Ding, Jundong Li, Xiaoming Zhai, Shaoyi Huang, Wei Niu, and Geng Yuan
    In Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing, 2026 Paper

    Problem: Backpropagation makes quantization-aware LLM training too memory-intensive for many devices.

    Contribution: ZeroQAT uses forward-only, zeroth-order optimization for low-bit training, including 13B models on an 8GB GPU.

    Key Insight: Avoiding backpropagation brings training memory requirements close to inference costs, enabling on-device adaptation.

  3. EMNLP
    A Dynamic Fusion Model for Consistent Crisis Response
    Xiaoying Song, Anirban Saha Anik, Eduardo Blanco, Vanessa Frias-Martinez, and Lingzi Hong
    In Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing, 2025 Paper

    Problem: Automated crisis responses can vary in style even when individual answers are useful.

    Contribution: A style-consistency metric and two-stage fusion method that scores and integrates candidate responses.

    Key Insight: Selecting and combining responses at the instance level improves both quality and stylistic consistency.

  4. COLM
    Multi-Agent Retrieval-Augmented Framework for Evidence-Based Counterspeech Against Health Misinformation
    Xiaoying Song*, Anirban Saha Anik*, Elliott Wang, Bryan Wang, Bengisu Yarimbas, and Lingzi Hong
    In Conference on Language Modeling, 2025 Paper

    Problem: Health counterspeech needs relevant, accurate evidence alongside constructive language.

    Contribution: A multi-agent RAG framework combining static and dynamic evidence for health misinformation intervention.

    Key Insight: Coordinating retrieval and response refinement improves factual accuracy, informativeness, relevance, and politeness.

  5. ISCRAM
    Dynamic Fusion of Large Language Models for Crisis Communication
    Xiaoying Song, Lingzi Hong, Anirban Saha Anik, and Vanessa Frias-Martinez
    In Proceedings of the International ISCRAM Conference, 2025 Paper

    Problem: Crisis communication must address information needs with relevant, empathetic, and actionable responses.

    Contribution: A comparison of prompting, RAG, and dynamic fusion of multiple LLMs for crisis-related social media queries.

    Key Insight: Dynamic fusion improves professionalism, actionability, empathy, and relevance by combining model responses.

Human-centered evaluation

Understanding model outputs and user reactions through human judgments, cognitive signals, and diagnostic benchmarks.

  1. NAACL
    Echoes of Discord: Forecasting Hater Reactions to Counterspeech
    Xiaoying Song, Sharon Lisseth Perez, Xinchen Yu, Eduardo Blanco, and Lingzi Hong
    In Nations of the Americas Chapter of the Association for Computational Linguistics, 2025 Paper

    Problem: A counterspeech response alone does not reveal whether a hater will return or remain hateful.

    Contribution: ReEco, a dataset of three-turn Reddit conversations, supports forecasting hater reentry and subsequent hatefulness.

    Key Insight: Counterspeech language is linked to later reactions, making conversational outcomes a useful evaluation target.

  2. COLING
    Assessing the Human Likeness of AI-Generated Counterspeech
    Xiaoying Song, Sujana Mamidisetty, Eduardo Blanco, and Lingzi Hong
    In International Conference on Computational Linguistics, 2025 Paper

    Problem: It is unclear how closely AI-generated counterspeech resembles human responses.

    Contribution: An evaluation of multiple generation strategies using both classifiers and human judges.

    Key Insight: Differences in style, politeness, and specificity make AI counterspeech readily distinguishable from human writing.

  3. iConference
    Analyzing the language of rejection: a study of user flagging responses to hate speech on Reddit
    Sharon Lisseth Perez, Xiaoying Song, and Lingzi Hong
    In Information Research an international electronic journal, 2025 Paper

    Problem: User responses that flag hate speech contain moderation signals that are difficult to identify automatically.

    Contribution: A dataset of Reddit flagging responses paired with hateful comments, with transformer-based classification and linguistic analysis.

    Key Insight: Distinctive patterns in user flagging language can inform hate speech moderation.

  4. Preprint
    Towards Human-Centered Natural Language Processing: A Survey of Cognitive Signal Integration
    Xiaoying Song, Anirban Saha Anik, Jinyu Liu, Qitao Tan, Geng Yuan, Song Fu, Eduardo Blanco, and Lingzi Hong
    arXiv preprint, 2026

    Problem: Cognitive signals offer a window into language processing, but their use across NLP is fragmented.

    Contribution: A survey connecting cognitive-signal datasets, preprocessing, tasks, and language-model integration methods.

    Key Insight: Eye tracking and EEG provide complementary perspectives for studying more interpretable, human-centered NLP.

  5. Preprint
    ST-Crisis: A Diagnostic Benchmark for Spatiotemporal Grounding in Crisis Communication
    Xiaoying Song, Thi Bich Ngan Do, Nikhitha Rayabarapu, Anirban Saha Anik, Jinyu Liu, Qitao Tan, Vanessa Frias-Martinez, and Lingzi Hong
    arXiv preprint, 2026

    Problem: Crisis responses must match evidence to the right location and time.

    Contribution: ST-Crisis pairs situational awareness posts with crisis queries to diagnose spatiotemporal grounding and reasoning.

    Key Insight: Both standard LLMs and grounded RAG systems still struggle to connect crisis evidence to where and when it applies.

Knowledge discovery & representation

Organizing information, learning representations, and uncovering patterns in scholarly data.

  1. JIS
    Comparative analysis of large language models’ performance in book classification tasks using Library of Congress Classification system
    Xiaoying Song, Pengcheng Luo, Jason Thomale, Oksana Zavalina, and Lingzi Hong
    Journal of Information Science, 2026 Paper

    Problem: Book classification requires LLMs to navigate a detailed library taxonomy using varied source material.

    Contribution: A comparative evaluation across models, input types, data sizes, and Library of Congress Classification levels.

    Key Insight: Diverse inputs help classification, while long texts and fine-grained categories remain challenging.

  2. ASIST
    A Hybrid Framework for Subject Analysis: Integrating Embedding-Based Regression Models with Large Language Models
    Jinyu Liu, Xiaoying Song, Diana Zhang, Jason Thomale, Daqing He, and Lingzi Hong
    In Association for Information Science and Technology, 2025 Paper

    Problem: LLM-generated subject headings can hallucinate labels or depart from a controlled vocabulary.

    Contribution: A hybrid framework using embeddings to predict heading counts and guide post-editing of LLM outputs.

    Key Insight: Combining prediction with vocabulary-aware editing yields more controlled subject assignments with fewer hallucinations.

  3. JOIS
    Academic collaboration recommendation based on graph neural network and multi-attribute embedding
    Guanghui Ye, Jinyu Wei, Qitao Tan, Chuan Wu, Xiaoying Song, and Songye Li
    Journal of Information Science, 2026 Paper

    Problem: Recommending collaborators requires modeling both scholarly relationships and researcher attributes.

    Contribution: MAH combines co-authorship and citation networks with multi-attribute researcher embeddings.

    Key Insight: Jointly representing different relationships and attributes improves collaboration recommendations on real-world datasets.

  4. MLJ
    An effective negative sampling approach for contrastive learning of sentence embedding
    Qitao Tan, Xiaoying Song, Guanghui Ye, and Chuan Wu
    Machine Learning, 2023 Paper

    Problem: Random negative sampling limits unsupervised contrastive learning of sentence embeddings.

    Contribution: nmCSE selects negatives through distance-based weighting and grid sampling.

    Key Insight: More deliberate negative selection improves sentence representations across seven semantic similarity datasets.

  5. JOI
    Research frontier detection and analysis based on research grants information: A case study on health informatics in the US
    Guanghui Ye, Cancan Wang, Chuan Wu, Ze Peng, Jinyu Wei, Xiaoying Song, Qitao Tan, and Lanqi Wu
    Journal of Informetrics, 2023 Paper

    Problem: Research frontier detection often focuses on publications and patents, leaving grant information underused.

    Contribution: A grant-based framework using adapted indicators and path mining to trace research topic evolution.

    Key Insight: Funding records offer another view of emerging research, identifying five frontiers in a US health informatics case study.
